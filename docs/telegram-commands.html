SensorSentinel — Telegram Quick Reference

Boat & jetty monitoring · @sensorsentinelchatbot · type / in the chat for this list on your phone

Checking on things

/status [device]Last reading from each device, with signal and age
/sensors [device]Every channel and what it reads — named or not
/infoWhat you own and how it is configured
/helpThe command list, in the chat
Only what you own. Every view is scoped to your own devices. With one device you can leave its name out of any command.

Watching live

/watch [device] [mins]Every reading as it arrives, ~1 per 30 s
/unwatch [device]Stop feeds — all of them, or one
Feeds expire. 30 minutes by default, then they stop by themselves — the catch for when you have walked out of signal. Stop on a feed stops that device; Another 30m resets it to 30 from now rather than adding.

Taking on a device

/claim <code> [name]Make it yours, and name it
/rename <code> <name>Give it a proper name
/release <device>Hand it to someone else
The code is on the screen. Press PRG on the device and it shows six characters for ten seconds. Claiming works even if the bot has never met you — no email, no invitation.
A claimed device cannot be claimed. Its owner must /release it first, so a code alone can never take a boat off someone.

Setting up sensors

/sensorname <ch> <name>Name a channel so it appears elsewhere
/sensoralert <Dn> <when>When a switch matters
/limits <ch> <low> [high]Alert outside a range
/limits <ch> offStop it alerting
ChannelWhat it is
bus0–bus7I²C sensors — battery monitors and the like
A0–A3Analog inputs, raw ADC counts
D0–D7Switch inputs — float switches, contacts
Naming is not arming. A named channel shows up in /status and stays silent until you give it a trigger or a limit.

Switch triggers

WordAlerts when
openthe contact opens — resolves when it closes
closedthe contact closes — resolves when it opens
changeeither way, neither called an alarm
offnever
1 means open. Inputs read 1 when nothing is pulling them down and 0 when closed to ground — so an unconnected input looks exactly like a switch sitting open.
To find which input something is on: toggle it, run /sensors again, and see which digit moved.

Where alerts go

/alertsTelegram, email, or both
/email on|offTurn email on or off
/mute [2h]Silence everything, every channel
/unmuteStart telling you again
You cannot leave yourself unreachable. /email off is refused while email is the only way to reach you.
A mute with a duration un-mutes itself. Without one it lasts until you say otherwise — and a muted account hears nothing at all, including a device going offline.

Setting up a new device

1. Power it up in range of a jetty gateway. It registers itself.
2. Press PRG — note the six characters on the screen.
3. /claim 676358 Renko
4. /sensors to see what it is reading.
5. /sensorname bus0 Boat battery then /limits bus0 12000 14500.
Check the units. /limits shows the live reading next to the limit you set, and says so if what you typed is already outside the range. A battery reading 12680 in millivolts needs 12000, not 12.

What the numbers mean

ShownMeaning
rssi −34Signal strength. Closer to zero is stronger; −120 is marginal
snr 14.3Signal above noise. Higher is better
12s agoAge of the reading, not the time now
** QUIET **Nothing heard for a while — treat every figure below it as stale
not alertingNamed, but nothing set to make it speak up

How it reaches you

boat → LoRa → jetty gateway → Tailscale → home
Readings travel one way only. The bot cannot ask a device anything, so every view is the most recent message it sent — usually under a minute old, and older if it has gone quiet. That is why the age sits beside the name.
Silence is the normal state. You hear when a reading crosses a limit, and again when it returns to normal. A device that stops reporting raises an alert of its own — a silent sensor otherwise looks identical to one saying all is well.
